CHAPTER 01

The Ruthenian Girl

Born in Ruthenia, in what is now Ukraine, she is traditionally known as Aleksandra Lisowska and described as the daughter of an Orthodox priest. Captured by Crimean Tatars during a raid, she was enslaved and taken to Istanbul. Her lively intelligence and resilient spirit would help her find her way in the sultan’s harem.

CHAPTER 02

Hürrem: The Joyful One

In the harem, she learned Turkish, music and the art of conversation. Her cheerful disposition earned her the name Hürrem, meaning “the joyful one”. Soon she attracted the attention of the young Sultan Süleyman.

CHAPTER 03

Love and Poetry

It was not only beauty that won Süleyman’s heart, but intelligence. Hürrem became his confidante and muse, and an influential adviser. Writing under the pen name Muhibbi, “the lover”, Süleyman dedicated poems to her, calling her his Istanbul, his Karaman, his Anatolia and Rumelia.

CHAPTER 04

A Remarkable Marriage

Süleyman broke with the practice followed by recent generations of Ottoman sultans: he freed Hürrem from slavery and legally married her. The marriage gave her an exceptional position at court and marked a turning point in the history of the dynasty.

CHAPTER 05

Political Influence

Hürrem became one of the most influential women of her time. She exchanged letters with European rulers, including the king of Poland, and took part in diplomatic affairs. Her position helped usher in the period later known as the Sultanate of Women.

CHAPTER 06

Haseki: The Benefactress

She devoted considerable wealth to charitable foundations. The Haseki Sultan complex in Istanbul included a mosque, educational institutions and a hospital. In Jerusalem she founded a public kitchen whose charitable work continued for centuries.

CHAPTER 07

The Struggle for Survival

Life in the harem also involved fierce dynastic rivalry. The succession system and the practice of fratricide threatened the lives of princes. Hürrem has often been accused of plotting against Grand Vizier İbrahim and Prince Mustafa, Süleyman’s eldest son, but her precise responsibility remains debated. Her story combines political ambition with concern for her children.

CHAPTER 08

Together for Eternity

Hürrem died in 1558, eight years before Süleyman. Today their separate mausoleums stand close together in the garden of the Süleymaniye Mosque in Istanbul. Their resting places recall one of the best-known relationships in Ottoman history.
